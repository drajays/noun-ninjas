🎨 The Adjective — Level Adventure

🎯 Your MissionAn Adjective is a word added to a noun to describe it, point it out, or tell how many / how much. Clear all 10 levels to become an Adjective Master! 👑

📖 Adjectives — Quick Guide

Adjective = a word added to a noun to describe it, point it out, or tell how many / how much.

5 Kinds of Adjectives:

  • 🎨 Quality — of what kind? (a large city)
  • ⚖️ Quantity — how much? (some rice)
  • 🔢 Number — how many? (five boys, the first day, each man)
  • 👉 Demonstrative — which? (this, that, these, those)
  • ❓ Interrogative — asks a question (what, which, whose)

How they are used: Attributive = before the noun (the lazy boy). Predicative = after a verb like is (the boy is lazy).

Comparison (3 degrees): Positive → Comparative → Superlative — sweet → sweeter → sweetest. Long words use more / most. Irregular: good → better → best, bad → worse → worst.

🅰️ Articles — Level Adventure

🎯 Your MissionThe little words a, an and the are Articles. a/an = any one (Indefinite); the = a particular one (Definite). Clear all 8 levels to become an Article Ace! 👑 Stuck? Tap 💡 Hint on any question.

📖 Articles — Quick Guide

There are two articles: a / an (Indefinite — any one) and the (Definite — a particular one).

a or an? — go by SOUND:

  • an before a vowel sound: an apple, an egg, an hour, an honest man
  • a before a consonant sound: a boy, a university (yu-), a one-rupee note (w-)

Use "the": for a particular/already-known thing (the book you want); unique things (the sun, the sky); whole class (the cow is useful); superlatives (the best); ordinals (the first); instruments (the flute); rivers/seas/ranges/holy books (the Ganga, the Himalayas, the Ramayana).

No article: uncountable/abstract in general (Gold is precious), plurals in general (Children like sweets), meals (have lunch), languages (speak English), and school/bed/church/hospital used for their purpose (go to school).

🧑 Pronouns — Level Adventure

🎯 Your MissionA Pronoun is a word used instead of a noun, so we don't repeat it. "Hari is ill, so he stays home." Clear all 10 levels to become a Pronoun Pro! 👑 Stuck? Tap 💡 Hint.

📖 Pronouns — Quick Guide

A Pronoun stands in place of a noun (pro-noun = "for a noun").

Personal pronouns — three persons:

  • 1st (speaker): I, we · 2nd (spoken to): you · 3rd (spoken of): he, she, it, they
  • Subject (doer): I, he, she, we, they · Object (receiver): me, him, her, us, them
  • Possessive: my/mine, your/yours, his, her/hers, our/ours, their/theirs, its

More kinds: Reflexive/Emphatic (myself, himself, themselves), Demonstrative (this, that, these, those), Indefinite (someone, anyone, nobody, all), Distributive (each, either, neither), Relative (who, which, that, whose, what), Interrogative (who? whom? whose? which? what?).

Golden rule: a pronoun must match its noun in number, gender & person — Rama → he, Sita → she, birds → they.

🏃 Verbs — Level Adventure

🎯 Your MissionA Verb is a word that tells or asserts something about a person or thing — usually a doing or being word. Hari laughs. The cat is black. Clear all 6 levels to become a Verb Victor! 👑 Tap 💡 Hint if stuck. (More advanced verbs coming soon!)

📖 Verbs — Quick Guide

A Verb tells or asserts something about a person or thing. It can tell:

  • what someone does — Hari laughs
  • what is done to them — the window is broken
  • what someone is — glass is brittle

A verb can be more than one word: were singing, has been found.

Transitive = its action passes over to an object (kicks the ball). Intransitive = the action stops with the doer (the baby sleeps). Some verbs take two objects — an indirect (person) and a direct (thing): gave him a watch.

Linking verbs (is, seem, become, grow, look) need a complement to complete the meaning: the baby seems happy.
